# IDP for US Drivers in Greece: The Legal Gray Zone Most Travelers Don't Know About

You've booked the flights to Athens, the ferry to Santorini, the cliffside hotel in Oia. You're picturing yourself winding along Crete's coastal roads or hopping islands by rental car. Here's what's confusing about Greece in 2026: **the law changed, but the rental counter often hasn't caught up**. Some US travelers fly in with just a US license and drive without issue. Others get refused at Athens airport, denied insurance after a fender-bender, or hit with a €1,000 fine they didn't see coming.

This guide explains what Greek law actually says about US drivers, what rental companies actually do, and where IDP Companion fits into a situation where the rules don't match the reality.

## Quick Answer: Do US Drivers Need an IDP in Greece?

**Legally — no longer strictly required. Practically — yes, get one.**

In November 2021, Greece passed Law 4850 (Article 25, Paragraph 3), which recognized US driver's licenses as valid for short-term tourist driving without an International Driving Permit. On paper, this means you can rent and drive a car in Greece with just your US license.

In reality, three things complicate this:

- **Rental companies set their own policies.** Many still require an IDP regardless of the law change, especially smaller and island-based agencies.
- **Insurance providers reference older standards.** If you have an accident, your rental insurance company may still ask for the IDP — and void coverage if you don't have one.
- **Police awareness varies.** Officers at rural checkpoints may not be up to date on the 2021 change and can issue fines that you'll have to contest.

**Without an IDP, you risk:**
- Refusal at the rental counter — especially during high season when companies are picky
- A €1,000 fine if a police officer hasn't internalized the law change (the pre-2021 standard fine, now legally questionable but still reported)
- Voided insurance after an accident if the policy references the older convention
- Hours of frustration trying to explain the new law to a counter agent who's heard it before

## Why Your US License Alone Creates Friction in Greece

Three reasons that rental counters and insurers cite, even when the law has technically moved on:

**The legal gray zone.** Law 4850 created a recognition pathway for US licenses, but didn't update every operational standard in the rental industry. Many car rental contracts were written under the previous law and still reference IDP as a requirement. Local agents at airport counters in Athens or Heraklion read from those contracts, not from updated legal interpretations.

**The insurance reason.** This is the strongest practical argument. Your rental car insurance policy includes a clause requiring proper documentation under "applicable law and rental contract." If the rental contract still requires an IDP and you signed it without one, you've technically violated the contract. After an accident, the insurer reviews the contract first. If they find a violation, coverage can be voided — leaving you personally liable for car damage, third-party damages, and medical costs.

**The translation reason.** Your US license is in English only. Greek rental agents on Milos or Folegandros at 11pm — running their family's rental shop with limited English — may simply prefer the IDP because they recognize the format. A multilingual companion document accomplishes the same goal: making your license data instantly readable.

## Greece Driving Rules US Drivers Should Know

A few rules that surprise drivers from the US:

- **Driving side: RIGHT.** Same as the US, no adjustment needed.
- **Speed limits:** 50 km/h (31 mph) urban, 90 km/h (56 mph) rural roads, 110 km/h (68 mph) on highways, 130 km/h (81 mph) on motorways. Strictly enforced with cameras on Athens-Thessaloniki E75 highway.
- **Alcohol limit:** 0.05% BAC for experienced drivers. **0.02% for new drivers (less than 3 years of license)** and motorcyclists. Stricter than most US states.
- **Phone use while driving:** banned, including hands-on use at red lights. Fines €100.
- **Greek alphabet on signs:** major roads have dual signage (Greek + Latin transliteration), but secondary roads — especially on islands — are Greek-only. This is the biggest navigation challenge for US tourists.
- **Roundabouts:** Greek roundabout etiquette is unusual — sometimes vehicles already in the roundabout yield to entering traffic, opposite of most other countries. Watch local behavior before assuming.
- **Aggressive city driving:** Athens traffic is famously chaotic. Motorcyclists weave between cars and lanes are aspirational rather than enforced.
- **Island roads:** narrow, often single-lane two-way, with cliff drops on Santorini, Folegandros, and Amorgos. Match your car size to the island.

## Greek Alphabet on Road Signs: The Real Navigation Problem

This is the killer practical issue Greek tourism boards rarely mention upfront. Major highways and tourist routes have bilingual signage — Greek and Latin transliteration — but as soon as you turn off onto secondary roads, especially on islands, the signs become Greek-only.

**Real examples US drivers encounter:**
- The road to your villa is signed "Καλαμωτή" but your reservation says "Kalamoti"
- A detour sign reads "ΕΡΓΑ" (works/construction) — easy to miss without alphabet recognition
- Speed limit signs are universal numerals, but warning signs about hazards or village limits are Greek text
- GPS apps usually transliterate, but the road signs you see don't match your screen

**What helps:**
- Google Maps offline downloads with Greek place names enabled
- A printed list of your destinations in both Greek and Latin script
- Taking photos of your destination written in Greek before leaving the hotel
- Using IDP Companion as a familiar bilingual reference at gas stations and ferry terminals

This is one specific area where a multilingual companion document earns its $35 — Greek small-town interactions are smoother when your documents are in formats that match what locals expect.

## Renting a Car in Greece as a US Driver

Major chains operate at Athens (ATH), Thessaloniki (SKG), Heraklion (HER on Crete), Rhodes (RHO), Corfu (CFU), Santorini (JTR), and Mykonos (JMK). Common American-friendly rental scenarios:

- **Hertz, Avis, Budget, Sixt, Europcar** — international chains, generally accept US license alone since 2021 law, but some branches still ask for IDP
- **Local mainland chains** — varies; many require IDP regardless of law change
- **Island family-run shops** — least predictable; some require IDP, some don't, often depends on the agent on duty
- **ATV/scooter rentals** (popular on Mykonos, Santorini, Naxos) — usually require less documentation but voided insurance is common

**Practical tips for US drivers:**
- Book online with a major chain in advance, especially July-August. Walk-ins during peak season have triple markup or no availability.
- Photograph the vehicle on pickup including odometer reading. Greek rental disputes about damage are common and photographic evidence is your protection.
- Always select Collision Damage Waiver and Theft Protection. The standard insurance often has high deductibles that void with any small documentation issue.
- A €1,000 deposit hold on your credit card is standard. Use a card with at least €2,500 available limit before pickup.
- Refuel before returning. Rental gas stations often charge 2-3x normal pump prices.
- For island hopping with multiple rental cars, keep all rental contracts together — disputes about earlier rentals can affect later bookings.

## FAQ

### If Greece changed the law in 2021, why do rental companies still ask for an IDP?

Two reasons. First, rental contracts are slow to update — many were written under the old law and still reference IDP requirements. Second, rental companies set their own policies independent of the law. They can require an IDP as a contractual condition even if Greek law doesn't. The 2021 change reduced government enforcement, but didn't eliminate rental industry practices.

### Can I get an IDP after I arrive in Greece?

No. International Driving Permits must be issued in your country of residence before you travel. For US drivers, only AAA and AATA are authorized. AAA processes IDPs at most branches in 10–15 minutes for $20. There is no Greek equivalent issued to foreigners.

### Is IDP Companion the same as the AAA International Driving Permit?

No. The AAA IDP is a government-recognized document under the 1949 Geneva Convention. IDP Companion is a multilingual translation document — it helps reduce friction at rental desks and informal verifications, but is not a legal substitute. Many US travelers carry both: AAA IDP for legal compliance, IDP Companion as a more travel-friendly translation aid.

### Do I need an IDP for the Greek islands specifically?

The legal answer is the same as for the mainland — under 2021 Law 4850, your US license is sufficient. The practical answer is that island rental shops are more likely to require an IDP than mainland chains. Family-run shops on Naxos, Milos, or Folegandros often haven't internalized the 2021 change and still operate under old policies. For islands, the IDP is more practically necessary than legally required.

### How long is IDP Companion valid?

Choose between 1 year ($35), 3 years ($45), or 5 years ($55). The validity is tied to your US license — if your domestic license expires, the companion expires with it. Many travelers choose the 3-year option for trips spanning multiple summers.

### What happens if I drive in Greece without an IDP and get stopped?

Under Law 4850 (2021), your US license should be sufficient. In practice, some officers — especially in rural areas — may not be familiar with the change and issue fines €100-300 (or invoke the older €1,000 standard). Contesting requires Greek-language paperwork and can take months. Carrying any form of additional translation document — official IDP or IDP Companion — significantly reduces friction during these stops.

### Will my US car insurance cover me in Greece?

Almost certainly not. US auto insurance policies (Geico, Progressive, State Farm, etc.) generally exclude international rentals. Credit card rental insurance (Visa, Amex, Capital One) varies wildly — some explicitly exclude Greece, some have geographic restrictions, and most have limits like 31-day maximum or specific car class requirements. Always select the in-rental Collision Damage Waiver and Theft Protection unless you have written confirmation from your card issuer that Greek rentals are covered.

### Can I rent an ATV or scooter on the Greek islands with just my US license?

Technically yes for cars (under 2021 law), but for motorcycles and ATVs the rules are stricter. You generally need a motorcycle endorsement on your US license to rent anything over 50cc. Many island scooter shops rent under-50cc bikes to anyone with any driver's license, but insurance is often non-existent on these rentals — and Greek hospital bills after motorbike accidents routinely exceed $10,000.

### What about driving from Greece into Albania or North Macedonia?

If you cross into a non-EU country with your Greek rental, the destination country's driving requirements apply. Albania accepts most international licenses including US. North Macedonia requires an IDP. Most Greek rental companies prohibit cross-border travel without explicit advance permission and additional insurance — check your contract before driving across the border.

### What if I'm staying in Greece longer than a typical tourist stay?

US tourists can drive on a US license + IDP for up to 6 months under standard tourist visa rules. Beyond that, residency triggers Greek licensing requirements through the local KEP (Citizen Service Center). Greece does have a license exchange agreement with some US states but not all — check with the local US consulate for your specific state's reciprocity status.
