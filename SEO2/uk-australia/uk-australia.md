## Quick Answer

**IDP legally required for UK photocard holders:** No — Australia requires a valid licence in English or accompanied by an English translation. UK photocard licences satisfy this requirement directly. No IDP needed for tourist visits.

**IDP required for UK paper licence holders:** Yes — old-style pink or green paper licences must be accompanied by an IDP or certified English translation. Most rental agencies will not accept a paper licence alone.

**Verdict:** Documentation is the simplest part of driving in Australia as a UK visitor. What requires preparation is understanding the point-to-point camera system, the phone laws, the double demerit periods, and the wildlife risk in regional driving.

## Why Australia Is Simple on Documentation and Complex on Everything Else

**The three-month tourist window**
Most Australian states allow temporary visitor visa holders to drive on a valid overseas English-language licence for the duration of their visit without conversion. NSW and Victoria set an explicit three-month limit from the date of entry; other states are more flexible while visitor visa status is maintained. If you're on a Working Holiday Visa (subclass 417 or 462) rather than a tourist visa, you're considered a temporary resident rather than a visitor — conversion timelines apply sooner. Know which visa you hold and check the rules for the specific states you'll be driving in.

**The paper licence gap**
Old-style UK paper licences are not accepted alone. Australian states require an English-language licence or an English translation — the paper licence itself is in English, but rental agencies and police in most states require it to be accompanied by a certified translation or IDP to confirm its validity and document class. Hertz, Avis, Sixt, and Budget Australia all document this requirement in their rental conditions.

**The digital licence situation**
Australia does not accept digital licences, smartphone-based licence images, or photocopies. The physical original must be present. UK visitors who've become accustomed to storing their licence on their phone as a backup should ensure the physical card is in their wallet before boarding.

## Driving Rules in Australia (2026)

Rules are state-level in Australia — what follows covers the national baseline with key state variations.

**Driving side:** Left — same as the UK, with right-hand steering wheel. Roundabouts work identically to UK roundabouts: traffic already on the roundabout has priority; signal left to exit.

**Urban speed limit:** 50 km/h (~31 mph) in most built-up areas; 40 km/h in school zones during school hours (actively enforced with cameras); 10 km/h in shared zones marked by signage

**Open road / rural highway:** 100 km/h (~62 mph) as default where no other sign is posted

**Motorway (freeway):** 100–110 km/h depending on state and posted limit; Victoria caps most freeways at 100 km/h; NSW and Queensland allow 110 km/h on some routes

**Alcohol limit:** 0.05% BAC for full licence holders; 0.00% for learner and provisional (P-plate) licence holders; 0.02% for some vehicle categories (heavy vehicles, buses)

**Phone use — the strictest rule you'll encounter:**
- Handheld use while driving: illegal across all states, including at red lights and in traffic queues with the engine running
- Touching the phone to check a notification, change music, or look at a map while stationary: illegal in Victoria, NSW, Queensland, South Australia, and Western Australia
- Bluetooth and hands-free calls are permitted; phone must be mounted in a cradle if used for navigation
- Camera-based phone detection is in use in NSW and Victoria — AI cameras can detect phone use regardless of whether an officer is present
- Fine in Victoria: AUD 555 (~£280) + 5 demerit points for any phone use; in NSW: AUD 356 + 5 demerit points

**Seatbelts:** Mandatory for all occupants; driver liable for unbelted passengers under 16; fine per person not buckled; child restraints compulsory and must be age-appropriate

**Keep left rule:** On roads with a speed limit above 80 km/h, drivers must keep left unless overtaking. On multi-lane roads with a speed limit of 90 km/h or above, driving in the right lane without overtaking is an offence.

**Double demerit periods:** In NSW, ACT, and Queensland, demerit point values double during designated holiday periods (Easter, Christmas, school holiday long weekends). A speeding infringement that would normally cost 3 demerit points costs 6 during these periods. In Queensland, double demerits apply year-round for repeat mobile phone, speeding, seatbelt, and motorcycle helmet offences within 12 months.

## The Wildlife Risk — Worth a Separate Section

Australian road authorities publish collision data that most travel guides summarise as "watch out for kangaroos." The actual numbers are more specific.

Kangaroos, wombats, wallabies, and emus are most active in the two hours after dusk and the hour before dawn. On regional roads in Queensland, NSW, Victoria, South Australia, and Western Australia, animal crossings during these windows are frequent enough that local advice is consistent: don't drive regional roads at night if you can avoid it. This isn't a precaution born of excessive caution — a large grey kangaroo can weigh 80 kg and stand 1.5 metres tall. A collision at 100 km/h causes severe vehicle damage.

**Rental insurance and wildlife:**
Most standard CDW rental policies cover wildlife collision damage. Some budget operators exclude it or charge a higher excess specifically for animal strikes. Check your rental agreement explicitly before departure — particularly if driving in the Northern Territory, outback Queensland, or the routes west of Adelaide.

**If an animal appears on the road:**
Brake, don't swerve. The instinct to swerve can take the vehicle off the road entirely, which causes more harm than the collision would have. Brake firmly and hold your line.

**Carcasses on the road:**
Dead animals on Australian roads are not immediately cleared in rural areas and can remain on the surface for days. After dark, these are a significant secondary hazard. They're often visible only when you're very close.

## FAQ

**Do I need an IDP to drive in Australia with a UK photocard licence?**
No. Australian states require a valid driving licence in English or accompanied by an English translation. A UK photocard licence satisfies this directly. An IDP is not a legal requirement for UK photocard holders driving as tourists.

**What about UK paper licences?**
Paper licences — the old green or pink ones — must be accompanied by an IDP or certified English translation. Most rental agencies in Australia list this requirement explicitly. A paper licence alone will not be accepted.

**How long is my UK licence valid in Australia?**
For tourist visits, most states allow driving on an overseas licence for the duration of the visit. NSW and Victoria specify a three-month limit from the date of entry. If your visa status changes to permanent residency, conversion to an Australian state licence is required within three months.

**Why is the phone law so strict in Australia?**
Australian road safety authorities have focused on distracted driving as a primary cause of fatal crashes since the mid-2010s. Victoria and NSW introduced AI-based camera detection systems that photograph vehicle interiors and automatically flag phone use. The camera doesn't require a police officer present — it operates continuously and processes images at enforcement centres. Touching your phone at a red light is the same offence as using it while moving.

**What are point-to-point cameras?**
Unlike fixed-point speed cameras that measure your speed at a single location, point-to-point systems (also called average-speed cameras) record your entry time at one camera and your exit time at another, several kilometres away. If your average speed over that distance exceeds the posted limit, a fine is issued regardless of your speed at any individual point. They're used extensively in NSW on the M1 and several other routes, and on the Pacific Highway. Slowing for a camera and speeding between cameras doesn't help.

**When should I not drive in regional Australia?**
Dawn and dusk — specifically the two hours after sunset and the hour before sunrise — are when kangaroos, wombats, and other wildlife are most active on roads. Local advice across Queensland, NSW, Victoria, South Australia, and Western Australia is consistent: avoid regional roads during these windows if you can. If you must drive, reduce speed significantly and use high beams where there's no oncoming traffic.

**Can I drive in Australia on a Working Holiday Visa?**
Working Holiday Visa (subclass 417 or 462) holders are considered temporary residents rather than tourists. The licence validity rules are the same — an English-language overseas licence is valid — but some state-level rules on conversion timelines may apply differently. Check with the transport authority in the specific state you'll be based in.

**Does IDP Companion help if my UK licence is lost in Australia?**
If your physical UK licence is lost, you'll need to contact the DVLA and your home country's licence-issuing authority for a replacement. IDP Companion can provide a reference document showing your licence details — useful for communicating with rental agencies or police while you wait for a replacement. It is not a substitute for the physical licence as far as Australian law is concerned.
