## Why the Dubai–Abu Dhabi Split Exists

**The federal position**
The UAE's Markhoos initiative, expanded to 52 countries in 2025, formally recognises tourist drivers from approved countries — including the UK — as eligible to drive on their home English-language licence without an IDP for the duration of their tourist visa. This is the national framework. In Dubai it is applied consistently at major rental agencies.

**The Abu Dhabi divergence**
Abu Dhabi has operated its own enforcement practices around foreign licences that predate and run alongside the federal framework. Abu Dhabi Police communications have historically stated that visitors must carry an IDP alongside their national licence. Budget's Abu Dhabi booking conditions list IDP as a requirement for all non-GCC holders. Other Abu Dhabi agencies follow similar policies. This isn't a contradiction of Markhoos — it's a stricter local interpretation that has persisted through multiple framework updates.

**The insurance dimension**
UAE insurance companies underwriting rental fleets have historically preferred IDP documentation for claims processing. The absence of an IDP when a claim is filed — particularly in Abu Dhabi — can complicate or delay settlement. Your UK licence is in English, which reduces but does not eliminate this risk in Abu Dhabi specifically.

**The practical implication**
Dubai-only trip: UK photocard alone is practically sufficient. Any itinerary that includes Abu Dhabi — Grand Mosque, Yas Island, Al Ain, E11 highway driving — carry a translation document before you cross the emirate boundary.

## Driving Rules in UAE (2026)

**Driving side:** Right — opposite to the UK; requires adjustment, particularly at first junctions after pickup

**Residential and small streets:** 40 km/h

**Main urban roads:** 60–80 km/h depending on posted signs

**Highways:** 100–120 km/h standard; select Abu Dhabi highways (E11) up to 140 km/h

**Speed camera buffer:** Dubai and most emirates — 20 km/h buffer before radar triggers. Abu Dhabi — zero buffer since 2018; any speed over the posted limit triggers a fine with no grace

**Alcohol limit:** Zero tolerance — 0.00% BAC across all seven emirates without exception. Any detectable blood alcohol while driving is a criminal offence. Mandatory jail time. No threshold, no rounding, no margin

**Phone use:** Handheld strictly prohibited; Bluetooth/car system permitted; AED 200–1,000 + 4–6 black points for violation

**Seatbelts:** Mandatory all occupants including rear seats; AED 400 + 4 black points per person not buckled

**Children:** Under 13 not permitted in front seat; ages 4–8 must use approved child seats

**Left lane on highways:** Reserved for fast-moving traffic; driving slowly in the left lane is a fineable offence (AED 400)

**Tailgating:** AED 400 + 4 black points; minimum 3-second following gap required by law

## FAQ

**Do I need an IDP to drive in the UAE with a UK photocard licence?**
At the federal level, no. The UAE's Markhoos initiative covers UK licence holders as tourists — a valid English-language UK photocard is sufficient without an IDP in Dubai and under federal law generally. Abu Dhabi applies stricter emirate-level enforcement; many rental agencies there and Abu Dhabi Police have historically required IDP documentation regardless.

**What's the practical rule if my trip covers Dubai and Abu Dhabi?**
Carry an IDP or translation document. The journey between the two takes under two hours and crosses an internal boundary with different enforcement expectations. IDP Companion costs less than the Salik tolls on that same journey.

**Is IDP Companion the same as a government IDP?**
No. A government IDP is a formal booklet issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention by an authorised UK body (Post Office or PayPoint). IDP Companion is a private multilingual translation document presenting your UK licence details in Arabic and other languages. For Abu Dhabi specifically, verify with your rental agency whether they accept a translation document or require the government-format IDP booklet.

**What does zero BAC tolerance actually mean in UAE?**
It means any detectable alcohol in your blood while driving is a criminal offence. There is no minimum threshold, no rounding, no margin. Alcohol is served in licensed hotel venues across Dubai and Abu Dhabi — UAE law distinguishes between consumption and driving. If you drink at dinner, do not drive that night.

**How do speed cameras work differently in Dubai vs Abu Dhabi?**
Dubai's cameras trigger only when you exceed the posted limit by more than 20 km/h — a tolerance buffer built into the system. Abu Dhabi has run zero-tolerance enforcement since 2018: the camera triggers at 1 km/h over the posted limit. Identical road behaviour — 105 km/h in a 100 km/h zone — is unpunished in Dubai and fined in Abu Dhabi.

**How do I pay a UAE speed camera fine?**
Fines are linked to the vehicle plate and issued to the rental company within approximately 48 hours. The agency charges the amount to your card and sends a notification. You can also check and pay directly via the Dubai Police app or the Abu Dhabi Police website using the plate number.

**Can I use my UK licence to pick up in Dubai and drive to Abu Dhabi?**
Yes — the road is open and the route is straightforward. The documentation recommendation changes when you cross into Abu Dhabi's jurisdiction. Pick up in Dubai with your UK licence; carry IDP Companion before you turn onto the E11 toward Abu Dhabi.

**Is driving in Dubai manageable for UK drivers?**
Yes, with the right-hand traffic adjustment. Roads are wide, well-signed in English and Arabic, and navigation apps work reliably. The left-lane-for-fast-traffic convention on highways is the biggest behavioural shift from UK driving habits. Traffic in central Dubai during peak hours (7–9am, 5–8pm Sunday–Thursday) is heavy; off-peak and outside the city it's straightforward.
