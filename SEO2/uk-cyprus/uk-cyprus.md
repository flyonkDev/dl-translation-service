## The Three Things That Actually Catch UK Drivers in Cyprus

**The red plates situation**
Rental cars in Cyprus carry bright red number plates instead of the standard white-and-yellow used by private vehicles. This is a nationally standardised system and it makes tourist rental cars immediately identifiable to police and other road users. It doesn't mean you'll be treated worse — Cypriot police are generally described as more lenient with tourists at minor infractions. But it does mean you're visible in a way you're not when driving your own car at home. Drive accordingly.

**The fifteen-day fine rule**
Speed camera and other traffic fines in Cyprus must be paid within fifteen days. The payment portal is jccsmart.com, and payment can also be made at local banks and police stations. If unpaid after fifteen days, the fine increases by fifty percent. After a further fourteen days, the case is referred to court. The rental company receives the fine notice and is legally required to provide your details to police — you'll receive correspondence at your home address in the UK. By the time it arrives, the fifteen-day window may already have passed.

**The Northern Cyprus insurance gap**
The island of Cyprus is divided by a UN Buffer Zone — the Green Line — separating the Republic of Cyprus (south) from the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus (north). Crossing between the two is possible at designated checkpoints. The problem: most car rental agreements in the Republic of Cyprus explicitly exclude coverage north of the Green Line. Driving a rental car into Northern Cyprus without specific insurance authorisation voids your policy. If something happens there — accident, breakdown, theft — you're personally liable. Some specialist insurers and a small number of rental companies offer cross-line coverage; verify in writing before crossing.

## Driving Rules in Cyprus (2026)

**Driving side:** Left — same as the UK, with right-hand steering wheel. Roundabouts work identically to UK roundabouts: traffic already on the roundabout has priority; signal right entering, left exiting.

**Urban speed limit:** 50 km/h (~31 mph); 30 km/h in marked school and pedestrian zones

**Rural road speed limit:** 80 km/h (~50 mph)

**Motorway speed limit:** 100 km/h (~62 mph) maximum; 65 km/h minimum

**Alcohol limit:** 22 µg/100ml of breath for experienced drivers (equivalent to approximately 0.05% BAC); 9 µg/100ml for drivers with fewer than three years' experience and motorcyclists — effectively near-zero

**Phone use:** Handheld strictly prohibited including when stationary with engine running; fine €150; hands-free permitted

**Seatbelts:** Mandatory for all occupants; €85 per person not buckled; children under 12 or shorter than 150 cm must use appropriate child seats

**Speed camera tolerance:** Within urban areas, 10% above limit plus 2 km/h before fine triggers; on motorways, 20% above limit before fine triggers (e.g., fine triggers at 121 km/h on a 100 km/h motorway)

**Radar detectors:** Prohibited — possession of a radar detector device is an offence regardless of whether it's active

**Overtaking:** On motorways and intercity roads, overtaking must be done on the right; the far-right lane is reserved for overtaking only and must be vacated after use; overtaking on the left is prohibited

**Right of way at uncontrolled intersections:** Traffic from the right has priority — the same rule as continental Europe, which feels counter-intuitive on a left-hand traffic island; proceed with caution at unmarked junctions

## Renting a Car in Cyprus

**Hertz Cyprus:** Accepts UK photocard licence without IDP. Operates at Larnaca (LCA) and Paphos (PFO) airports. Minimum age 21; young driver surcharge under 25 at some categories. Credit card required for deposit.

**Avis Cyprus:** Same photocard policy. Available at LCA and PFO. Minimum age 23 for standard vehicles at some locations.

**Sixt Cyprus:** Photocard accepted. Minimum age 21. Northern Cyprus coverage excluded from standard rental — verify before crossing.

**Local agencies (Paphos, Limassol, Nicosia, Ayia Napa):** Generally accept UK photocard without IDP. Some smaller operators request additional documentation for paper licences. Pricing is competitive with international chains.

**Practical tips for renting in Cyprus:**
- Automatic transmission is widely available and no more expensive than manual at most agencies; book preference in advance for peak season
- Credit card for deposit is mandatory across all agencies; holds of €500–€1,500 are standard
- Northern Cyprus: most standard rental agreements explicitly exclude coverage north of the Green Line; if you plan to cross, request written confirmation of coverage from the agency before departure — this may require a specialist policy
- Fuel: unleaded (95 and 98 octane) and diesel available; most stations accept card; rural stations in the Troodos mountains may be cash-only and have limited hours
- Summer temperatures in Cyprus regularly exceed 40°C — check tyre condition, carry water, and take rest breaks on longer journeys; air conditioning significantly increases fuel consumption
- Animals on rural roads: stray dogs, cats, and goats appear without warning, particularly in the Troodos foothills and near villages in the Paphos district

## FAQ

**Do I need an IDP to drive in Cyprus with a UK photocard licence?**
No. Cyprus recognises valid UK driving licences for tourist use. An IDP is not a legal requirement for UK photocard holders in the Republic of Cyprus.

**What about UK paper licences?**
Paper licences are generally accepted but less consistently than photocards. Some rental agencies request a supplementary IDP alongside a paper licence. If you hold an older green or pink paper licence, carry an IDP or translation document as a precaution.

**How does the alcohol limit work — is it the same as the UK?**
Cyprus expresses its limit in breath units: 22 µg per 100ml of breath for experienced drivers. This is approximately equivalent to 0.05% BAC — stricter than England and Wales (0.08%) and equal to Scotland (0.05%). For drivers with fewer than three years' experience, the limit is 9 µg/100ml — effectively near-zero. The conversion from UK percentage to breath units catches some drivers off guard.

**What happens if I get a speed camera fine?**
The notice goes to the rental company, which provides your details to Cypriot police. You'll receive correspondence at your UK address. You have fifteen days to pay via jccsmart.com or at a local bank. After fifteen days, the fine increases by fifty percent. After a further fourteen days, the case is referred to court.

**Can I drive into Northern Cyprus in my rental car?**
Crossing into Northern Cyprus is possible at designated Green Line checkpoints. However, most standard rental agreements from Republic of Cyprus agencies explicitly exclude coverage north of the Green Line. Driving there in a standard rental voids your insurance entirely. If you want to visit Northern Cyprus by car, request written confirmation from the agency that cross-line coverage is included — this usually requires a separate insurance product.

**Is driving in Cyprus genuinely easy for UK drivers?**
Yes — it is probably the most straightforward driving environment for UK visitors anywhere in Europe. Left-hand traffic, right-hand drive vehicles, roundabout rules identical to the UK, bilingual signage, and roads that are generally well-maintained on the main tourist routes. The Troodos Mountains involve hairpin switchbacks and steep gradients that require patience; the main coastal roads and motorways between cities are unchallenging.

**Why do rental cars have red number plates?**
Cypriot law requires rental vehicles to carry distinctive red licence plates instead of the standard white/yellow plates used on private vehicles. This makes rental cars immediately visible to police as tourist vehicles. Most Cypriot officers extend more patience to tourists at minor infractions — but the visibility also means you're more likely to be noticed if something is wrong.

**Is IDP Companion useful for Cyprus specifically?**
For Cyprus itself, the legal requirement is straightforward — UK photocard holders don't need one. IDP Companion is most useful for UK drivers who are combining Cyprus with other destinations in the same trip: UAE, Egypt, Turkey, or Morocco, where a translation document is either legally required or strongly recommended. One purchase covers all of them.
